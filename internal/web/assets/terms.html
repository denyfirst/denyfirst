Short, because a page nobody reads protects nobody. Using this service means agreeing to what follows.

Scan only what you are entitled to scan

By submitting a hostname you confirm that you own the server, administer it, or have permission from whoever does. A check is a real connection to a real machine, and the decision to make it is yours.

Rules about connecting to systems you do not control differ from country to country, and neither this service nor its author can tell you what applies where you are. That judgement stays with you.

Do not use this as a weapon

Not for reconnaissance against systems you have no business examining, not for generating load, not for automated bulk collection. Limits are in place and are set to permit ordinary use comfortably. Working around them is a use this service is not offered for.

The result is a description, not advice

A report describes what a server negotiated at one moment, graded against a published rule set. It is not a security audit, not a certification, and not a statement that anything is safe. It says what was measured, and it says what was not.

Every report names the limits of what it covers. Read that section: a short list of findings can mean a well-configured server or a scan that could not see very far, and the report tells you which.

That is not a formality. This is a project run by one person, and claiming otherwise would be the same kind of overstatement the tool exists to identify.

Findings belong to you

A finding describes a server's public configuration — something anyone connecting to it can observe. It is not reported to anyone, published, or kept. What you do with a result is yours to decide, including whether to tell the operator.
